Why I built RestLess

One person's app, built for my own shifts

I'm an HGV driver. My shifts are long, and most of the day I'm stuck behind the wheel on the motorway, barely moving for hours. I'd read that long unbroken stretches of sitting are bad for you, even if you exercise. And it doesn't take much to break them up: a couple of minutes moving every half hour.

My Apple Watch already has a Stand reminder, but it's easy to satisfy without really moving. Stand up, sit back down, ring closed. I wanted something that checked whether I'd actually got moving.

So I built one. RestLess watches your heart rate during a session. When you've been still for about 30 minutes, it gives you a tap: Time to Move. Then it waits to see whether your heart rate actually goes up. If it does, you get a Nice Work. If it doesn't, it doesn't pretend you did. It starts the clock again and nudges you later.

I've worn it on full shifts for weeks: on the motorway, and out in the yard when I'm covering the shunter, climbing on and off the tug all day. I fixed whatever broke until a whole shift ran clean.

A few things I want to be upfront about

  • You start a session yourself. watchOS only allows continuous heart-rate monitoring during a workout-style session, so RestLess runs one while you're using it.
  • That session credits your Activity rings. Exercise minutes and some Move calories count while it runs. It's never saved as a workout, but if you care about your rings, you should know.
  • Battery: I typically finish a 13-hour day with over half left, with Always On turned off.
  • No data leaves your watch. No account, no analytics, no tracking. It reads your heart rate and that's it.

The research behind the 30-minute idea is on the research page.

If you sit for long stretches at a desk, in a cab or on the sofa, I hope it helps you too. I read every email at support@restless.watch.
